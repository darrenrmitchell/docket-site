The problem

The care is happening. You just can't see it.

Your parent has a nurse or helper, a doctor and a list of medications. The information about all of it is in a dozen places, and most of it reaches you late.

"Did she take her pressure tablets?"

You find out about a missed dose days later, or not at all. Readings get written on scraps of paper, or not written down.

"What did the doctor say?"

Appointments come and go with no shared record. The next visit starts from memory, and the doctor sees one reading instead of a month of them.

"Is everybody up to date?"

Siblings, relatives who help pay, and the nurse each have part of the picture. You end up relaying updates by phone and WhatsApp, one person at a time.

Docket gives the person running the care one view of it. Daily readings, medications, appointments and alerts sit in one place, and the people around your parent stay informed without you chasing them.

How it works

Set up once with a person. Then it runs every day.

There's nothing to install for your parent or their nurse. You use Docket on your phone or computer.

Step 1

A welcome call to set up

A member of our team walks you through setup: conditions, medications, appointments, and the blood pressure and glucose limits that should trigger an alert. You review and confirm it in Docket.

Phone call + Docket
Step 2

A daily check-in on WhatsApp

Each day your parent, or their nurse or helper, gets a short check-in: today's readings and whether the medications were taken. They reply in the chat.

WhatsApp
Step 3

You see it, and hear when it matters

Responses land in Docket as they arrive. If a reading crosses your limits, or check-ins stop, you get an alert and a member of our team checks it with you.

Privacy and consent

Your family's health information stays your family's

Docket is designed to comply with Jamaica's Data Protection Act, 2020. Consent comes first, before any health information is collected.

  • Consent before anything elseThe patient agrees before check-ins begin. Where a patient can't consent, their caregiver consents on their behalf.
  • The patient decides what's keptChoose which information is stored and for how long.
  • Never soldHealth information isn't sold or shared with advertisers, and isn't disclosed to anyone without explicit authorisation.
  • Support for care, not a diagnosisDocket doesn't diagnose and doesn't replace your parent's doctor.

Docket is not an emergency service. In an emergency call 119 (police) or 110 (ambulance and fire).

Questions

Before you sign up

How much does Docket cost?

Founding households get founding terms, which we'll go through on your welcome call. There's nothing to pay to register your interest.

Does my parent need a smartphone?

They, or their nurse or helper, need WhatsApp. That's all. If someone usually helps your parent with their phone, that works too.

What equipment do we need?

A blood pressure monitor at home, and a glucometer if your parent is diabetic. Docket records the readings these take. We'll confirm what you need on the welcome call.

Where is Docket available?

Across Jamaica, from November 2026. Places are limited for the first group of founding households.

I live overseas. Can I use Docket?

Yes. You can join a Care Circle and receive the weekly summary. Docket works best when someone close by coordinates day-to-day care, and that person can be the coordinator while you stay informed.

Is Docket a medical service?

No. Docket helps families organise and keep track of care. It doesn't diagnose, prescribe, or replace your parent's doctor, and it isn't an emergency service.

What happens after I sign up?

We'll email you before launch. Founding households get a call to set up their Care Circle, and you'll receive a link to sign in to Docket.
